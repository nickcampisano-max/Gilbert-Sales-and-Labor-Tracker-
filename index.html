How this runs each week
  1. Open workbook — the latest .json from the shared Drive folder.
  2. Monday: pull last week's payroll (one week, Mon–Sun) and Sales by day, and next week's 7shifts draft. Drop them together.
  3. Check the cards, confirm any missing sales days, then Write in.
  4. Fix anything in Reconcile names before reading posted vs actual.
  5. In section 4, size the draft schedule and commit next week's sales number.
  6. Save workbook and Export for Drive; put both in the shared folder. The file is the record.

Notes — what this does and doesn't know

Labor % is hourly wages only. Toast Total Pay (regular + overtime, no tips) divided by net sales. Salaried staff, payroll taxes and benefits are not in it. House/terminal accounts are removed before anything else.

Two different rates. Actual cost uses each person's weighted average (total pay ÷ total hours), never their highest job code. Next week's schedule is priced at regular pay ÷ regular hours, so last month's overtime premium isn't baked into this week's estimate.

A name that doesn't match is a data problem, not a finding. Unreconciled people are listed and kept out of posted-vs-actual until linked. Someone who appears on the 7shifts schedule at 0 hours and still clocked in is a real “not posted”.

Requested time off is invisible. 7shifts shows it as an alert with no hours, and the export renders it as a blank cell — identical to “not scheduled”. A light schedule may be a cut, a vacation, or a departure; this page can't tell which.

Comparisons are not prescriptions. Who can close a station, skill mix and coverage needs are not in any file. Every suggestion carries its basis on the same line so it can be rejected in seconds.

No closed days are assumed. A date inside a sales export's range with no row is flagged for you to confirm once (closed, or a bad export) and then remembered.

Last year's same week is the week 364 days earlier — same fiscal week in a 13×4 year.
